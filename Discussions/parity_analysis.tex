\section{The Hamiltonian}

The system under study is a periodically driven Lipkin--Meshkov--Glick (LMG) model
coupled to a single bosonic cavity mode:
\begin{equation}
  H(t) = -\frac{4J}{N}\,S_z^2 \otimes I_\text{ph}
         + 2h(t)\,S_x \otimes I_\text{ph}
         + \hbar\omega_0\,I_\text{spin} \otimes a^\dagger a
         + \frac{2g}{\sqrt{N}}\,(a+a^\dagger)\otimes S_x ,
  \label{eq:ham}
\end{equation}
where $h(t)=h_0\cos(\omega t)$, $S_\alpha = \sum_i \sigma_\alpha^{(i)}/2$ are
collective spin-$S$ operators with $S=N/2$, and $a,a^\dagger$ are photon
ladder operators.  The system is initialized in
$\ket{\psi_0}=\ket{S_x{=}{+}S}\otimes\ket{0}_\text{ph}$.

\section{Exact Z\textsubscript{2} Symmetry of the Full Driven Hamiltonian}

\subsection{The conserved parity}

Define the spin-rotation operator
\begin{equation}
  \Pi_x \;=\; e^{i\pi S_x}\otimes I_\text{ph} .
\end{equation}
This is a rotation of the spin by $\pi$ about the $x$-axis, leaving the photon
sector untouched.  Under $\Pi_x$:
\begin{equation}
  S_x \;\longrightarrow\; S_x, \qquad
  S_y \;\longrightarrow\; -S_y, \qquad
  S_z \;\longrightarrow\; -S_z .
\end{equation}
Therefore $S_z^2\to S_z^2$, $S_x\to S_x$, and $(a+a^\dagger)\to(a+a^\dagger)$.
Checking each term of $H(t)$:
\begin{align}
  \Pi_x\,\bigl(-\tfrac{4J}{N}S_z^2\bigr)\,\Pi_x^\dagger
    &= -\tfrac{4J}{N}(-S_z)^2 = -\tfrac{4J}{N}S_z^2 \qquad\checkmark \\
  \Pi_x\,\bigl(2h(t)S_x\bigr)\,\Pi_x^\dagger
    &= 2h(t)S_x \qquad\checkmark \\
  \Pi_x\,\bigl(\hbar\omega_0\,a^\dagger a\bigr)\,\Pi_x^\dagger
    &= \hbar\omega_0\,a^\dagger a \qquad\checkmark \\
  \Pi_x\,\bigl(\tfrac{2g}{\sqrt{N}}(a+a^\dagger)S_x\bigr)\,\Pi_x^\dagger
    &= \tfrac{2g}{\sqrt{N}}(a+a^\dagger)S_x \qquad\checkmark
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{[\,\Pi_x,\,H(t)\,] = 0 \quad\text{for all }t.}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Consequence for the initial state}

The eigenvalues of $\Pi_x=e^{i\pi S_x}$ on $S_x$ eigenstates $\ket{m_x}$ are
$e^{i\pi m_x}=(-1)^{m_x}$ (for integer $m_x$ with $S$ integer).
The initial spin state $\ket{S_x{=}{+}S}$ has eigenvalue
$(-1)^{S}=(-1)^{N/2}$.  For $N=400$ this is $(-1)^{200}=+1$.

Because $[\Pi_x,H(t)]=0$ exactly, the parity eigenvalue is a constant of
motion.  The system is permanently confined to the \emph{even-$m_x$}
sector: $m_x \in \{0,\pm2,\pm4,\ldots,\pm S\}$ (for $N/2$ even).
The $\binom{N+1}{2}$ odd-$m_x$ spin states are \emph{permanently dark}.

\section{The Dicke Parity Is Broken by the Drive}

The standard Dicke-model parity operator is
\begin{equation}
  \Pi_D = e^{i\pi(a^\dagger a + J + J_z)}.
\end{equation}
Under $\Pi_D$, one finds $J_\pm \to -J_\pm$ (since $J_z$ shifts by $\pm1$), so
$S_x = (J_++J_-)/2 \to -S_x$, while
$e^{i\pi a^\dagger a}(a+a^\dagger)e^{-i\pi a^\dagger a}=-(a+a^\dagger)$.
Thus the coupling term $(a+a^\dagger)S_x\to(-(a+a^\dagger))(-S_x)=(a+a^\dagger)S_x$
is invariant.  However, the drive transforms as
\begin{equation}
  2h(t)S_x \;\xrightarrow{\Pi_D}\; -2h(t)S_x ,
\end{equation}
so $[\Pi_D, H(t)]\ne 0$ whenever $h(t)\ne 0$.
\emph{The Dicke parity is broken by the transverse drive.}

\section{The Coupling Is a QND Interaction for $S_x$}

The coupling Hamiltonian commutes with the spin observable it couples to:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl[H_\text{coup},\,I_\text{ph}\otimes S_x\bigr]
  = \frac{2g}{\sqrt{N}}(a+a^\dagger)\otimes[S_x,S_x] = 0 .
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{quantum-non-demolition} (QND) property: the coupling does
\emph{not} change the $S_x$ quantum number of the spin.  In the Heisenberg
picture:
\begin{equation}
  \dot S_x \Big|_{H_\text{coup}} = i[H_\text{coup},S_x] = 0 .
\end{equation}
The spin's $S_x$ dynamics is driven \emph{solely} by $H_\text{LMG}(t)$.
The cavity has \emph{no back-action} on $S_x$.

\subsection{Consequence: the cavity equation of motion}

In the Heisenberg picture the photon field satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \dot a = -i\omega_0\,a - i\frac{2g}{\sqrt{N}}\,S_x(t),
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where $S_x(t)$ evolves \emph{independently} of $a$ (due to the QND property).
Equation~\eqref{eq:eom} is that of a \emph{harmonically oscillator driven by an
external force} $F(t)\propto S_x(t)$.  The cavity mode can be displaced and can
accumulate coherent amplitude, but the driving force is not truly dissipative in
the quantum-thermodynamic sense.

\subsection{Why coherent driving does not thermalize the cavity}

For the cavity to thermalize to temperature $\beta^{-1}$, the coupling must
provide both \emph{heating} (stimulated absorption) and \emph{cooling}
(stimulated emission) with rates satisfying detailed balance.  These rates are
set by the bath spectral function
$\mathcal{S}(\nu)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\nu t}\langle S_x(t)S_x(0)\rangle_\text{bath}$.

Because the coupling conserves $S_x$ and the driving is Hamiltonian (not
dissipative), the cavity equation of motion~\eqref{eq:eom} admits only
\emph{unitary} dynamics.  An initially pure state remains pure.  More
concretely, starting from the vacuum $\ket{0}_\text{ph}$, the photon evolves
into a \emph{coherent state} that tracks the spin:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(t) = \langle a\rangle(t) = -i\frac{2g}{\sqrt{N}}
              \int_0^t dt'\,\langle S_x(t')\rangle\,e^{-i\omega_0(t-t')}.
\end{equation}
Even if $\langle S_x\rangle_\text{time-avg}=0$ (infinite-temperature spin
state), the photon state fluctuates around the vacuum but does not build up
a thermal occupation.  True thermalization requires the bath to provide
\emph{quantum noise} with the right spectral properties; for the QND coupling
this requires the many-body fluctuations of $S_x$ to act as a proper Markovian
heat bath.  For the finite LMG system this condition is generically \emph{not}
met: the correlation function $\langle S_x(t)S_x(0)\rangle$ is dominated by a
few Floquet frequencies (it does not decay to zero), so the spectral function
$\mathcal{S}(\nu)$ is a comb of delta functions rather than a smooth function of $\nu$.

\section{Summary and Physical Interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exact conserved parity (but acts only on spins)}:
        $\Pi_x = e^{i\pi S_x}\otimes I_\text{ph}$ is conserved for all $t$.
        The initial state has parity $+1$; only even-$m_x$ spin states are
        ever populated.  This halves the effective spin Hilbert space but does
        not directly restrict which photon states can be reached.

  \item \textbf{Broken Dicke parity}: The photon-spin Dicke parity
        $\Pi_D=e^{i\pi(a^\dagger a+J+J_z)}$ is broken by the drive $2h(t)S_x$.
        At low drive frequency the Floquet Hamiltonian $\approx$
        time-averaged $H$ \emph{does} approximately conserve $\Pi_D$, which
        forces the system to stay near the initial Dicke parity sector during
        most of the slow cycle.

  \item \textbf{QND coupling is the primary obstruction}:
        $[H_\text{coup}, S_x]=0$ means the cavity drives the spin's $S_z$ and
        $S_y$ components but \emph{never changes $S_x$}.  The coupling is a
        displacement of the photon conditioned on $S_x$, not a genuine
        energy-exchange channel.

  \item \textbf{Remedy}: Replace or augment the coupling with one that does
        not commute with the relevant spin observable, e.g.,
        $(a^\dagger J_- + a J_+)$ (Jaynes--Cummings type) or
        $(a+a^\dagger)S_z$.  The former exchanges quanta resonantly and
        satisfies detailed balance; it is the standard mechanism for cavity
        thermalization.  With the present $(a+a^\dagger)S_x$ coupling, the
        Jaynes--Cummings energy-exchange channel is absent in the rotating-wave
        approximation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Parity conservation \emph{is} relevant: the $\Pi_x$ symmetry is exact and
confines the spin to the even-$S_x$ sector, while the Dicke parity $\Pi_D$ is
only approximately conserved at low drive frequency.  However, the more
fundamental reason the cavity fails to thermalize is that the coupling
$H_\text{coup}\propto (a+a^\dagger)S_x$ is a QND interaction: it conserves $S_x$
and provides only coherent displacements of the photon.  Without a genuine
energy-exchange channel (or a truly Markovian spin bath in the thermodynamic
limit), the cavity cannot be driven to infinite effective temperature by the
spin alone.
